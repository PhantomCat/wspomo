A Pomodoro timer anchored to your work schedule

Most pomodoro apps start when you click Start. wspomo starts when your working day does — set your schedule once, and the day organizes itself.

Define your 9-to-5 (or 8-to-4, or shift start) and lunch once. From then on, pomodoro sessions run against your workday: pomodoros accumulate toward short breaks, the long break comes after the session counter you set, and the remaining minutes count down to your schedule, not to a random stopwatch.

What makes it different

  • Schedule-anchored pomodoros. Sessions sync to your workday start, lunch and end — staying consistent with real overtime and coffee interruptions.
  • Workday sync (optional, server-side). The day's chain lives on the server and keeps ticking even with the browser closed — the count-down shown on the site is the same time your waybar module or TUI client shows on another machine.
  • Multiple client frontends. A web page, a waybar plugin and a terminal client can all follow the same server-workday schedule in real time.
  • Yours to run. GPLv3, single Docker image, one PostgreSQL. Point it at your own work schedule and stay in full control of your data.

Use it as a local timer

Everything runs in your browser. No signup, no data leaving your device unless you choose otherwise.

Self-host it in one YAML

Docker compose: app, Postgres, Caddy. Schedule settings live per user; API tokens power headless clients like waybar and terminal.

Self-host in three steps

  • Clone the repository (GPLv3),
  • docker compose up with its own Postgres and Caddy,
  • point your domain at it and set your workday in the timer settings.
